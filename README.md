# Stress-Testing-Organizational-Stability


This project develops a structured stress-testing model to evaluate how workforce attrition, leadership turnover, and revenue pressure impact organizational stability.
                                                                    
                         The goal is to make human capital risk measurable, comparable, and actionable.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Workfroce metrics are often monitored operationally,, but not often translated into financial materiability. This model answers key questions such as 
- How resilient is the orgnaization under moderate vs severe stress?
- At what point should leadership intervene?

STRUCTURE

Defined:
Base Case: Normal workforce conditions
Modeate Stress: Elevated attrition and leadership turnover
Severe Stress: High volatility combined with revenue pressure

For each scenario, the model:

- Estimates workforce replacement cost
- Quantifies leadership instability cost
- Applies revenue shock
- Calculates total financial exposure
- Converts exposure into a 0–100 Stability Score
- Generates recommendation thresholds

The stability scoring is based on financial exposure as a percentage of annual revenue, groundign the output in materiality rather than relative normalization.

RESULTS

| Scenario        | Risk Exposure ($) | Stability Score | Recommendation                              |
| --------------- | ----------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Base            | 470,000           | 90              | Stable – Maintain strategy                  |
| Moderate Stress | 1,030,500         | 20              | Critical – Structural intervention required |
| Severe Stress   | 2,043,250         | 5               | Critical – Immediate stabilization required |

INSIGHT

The organization demonstrates high sensitivity to moderate workforce volatility. Under moderate stress, financial exposure becomes material enough to trigger structural intervention. Under severe stress, stability deteriorates rapidly. This indicates that retention and leadership continuity is an important stabilizing factor.

This framework enable proactive risk planning rather than a reactive response and allows the possibility for reducing attrition and improving leadership retention.
